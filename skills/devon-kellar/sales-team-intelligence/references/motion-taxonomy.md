# The eight sales motions, and how to evidence each

The tag answers one question: *how does this team actually sell?* Not what they sell, not who
to. Tag from this closed set — inventing a ninth motion for one unusual account destroys
comparability across the list, which is the whole point of tagging.

## The taxonomy

| Motion | What it tells you | What it looks like in the team's own words |
|---|---|---|
| **Outbound prospecting** | Dedicated prospecting roles exist | Prospecting-titled roles; "pipeline generation", "booked N meetings", "sourced my own pipeline" |
| **Email-led** | Outbound runs on sequenced email | "Email sequences", "multichannel cadences", "cold email campaigns", sequencing-platform fluency |
| **Phone-led** | Outbound runs on the phone | "Outbound calls", "dialing", "N calls a day", "telesales", dialer fluency |
| **Field** | In-person, territory-based | "Field sales", "territory", "regional/area sales", "outside sales", "on-site visits", travel-heavy |
| **Partner / channel** | They sell through others, not direct | "Channel sales", "partner manager", "alliances", "reseller", "distributor management" |
| **Account-based** | Concentrated effort on named accounts | "ABM", "named accounts", "strategic accounts", "key accounts", "target account list", "1:few" |
| **Inbound** | Selling against demand that arrives | "Inbound qualification", "demo qualification", "marketing-qualified", "inbound pipeline" |
| **Post-sale expansion** | Growth from existing customers | Account-management titles are sufficient alone — the role *is* the motion. Bonus: "renewals", "expansion", "upsell", "land-and-expand" |

**Post-sale expansion is the one exception to needing phrase evidence.** A senior account
manager's title tells you the motion by itself. Every other motion needs the words.

## Strength, and the cap

- **Primary** — what the team is built around. Usually one, sometimes two.
- **Secondary** — real but not central. Frequently none, and that is fine.
- **Three tags maximum**, primary and secondary combined.

The cap is not cosmetic. Motion tags exist to be scanned across a list of accounts; a team
carrying five tags is indistinguishable from a team carrying five tags, which is to say the
column stops discriminating. If more than three clear the bar, keep the three with the
strongest evidence.

**There is no trace tier.** A single mention in one person's past-role text is not weak
evidence — it is not evidence. Tagging it muddies every comparison downstream.

## The evidence bar

A motion may be tagged only if one of these holds:

1. **Two or more team members** show the signal, or
2. **One member** shows it in their **current-role** description, unhedged, or
3. The team is **one person** and their current role states the motion plainly

Then, every surviving tag must satisfy all of:

- **A verbatim quote from a named person.** No quote, no tag. This is the rule that makes the
  output defensible when a reader disagrees.
- **Current role only.** Headline, current-role description, or summary. Previous-role text is
  research context and nothing more.
- **No hedges.** These words around the motion phrase disqualify the quote: *also*,
  *sometimes*, *in past roles*, *previously*, *before that*, *earlier in my career*, *used to*.

## Worked examples

### A team that tags cleanly

A 40-person logistics provider. Four sales-shaped people found.

```
outbound prospecting (primary): Danielle O. — "I run outbound prospecting into mid-market
  3PLs, booking 8-12 discovery meetings per month."
phone-led (primary): Tom H. — headline reads "BDR | 80 dials a day into UK ecom warehousing."
account-based (secondary): Priya S. — "Own 12 named enterprise accounts end to end."
```

Three tags, three named people, three current-role quotes. A reader can challenge any one of
them and be answered.

### A team that must stay empty

An 11-person packaging manufacturer. Two sales-shaped people found. One is a "Sales
Administrator" whose description covers order processing. The other is a commercial director
whose summary says "earlier in my career I built an outbound function from scratch."

**Correct output: no motion tags.** The administrator shows no motion. The director's line is
past-role *and* hedged — it fails twice. The honest brief says a two-person commercial
function with no evidenced motion, which tells a reader something real: this is a
relationship or inbound business, approach accordingly.

The tempting mistake is tagging outbound prospecting off that director's sentence. It is
exactly one sentence about a job they no longer hold, and it would put this account
side-by-side with the logistics provider above, which genuinely runs outbound today.

### A team where the label lies

A 200-person software company returns nine sales-shaped people. Reading them: three are sales
operations (reporting, tooling, territory design), two are customer support with "customer" in
the title, four carry quotas.

**Team size is nine; the number that matters is four.** Say both. A brief reporting "9-person
sales team" invites a reader to conclude they are outbound-heavy when most of the nine never
speak to a prospect.

## Why the bar is this strict

Every rule above replaced a looser version that produced worse output:

- Allowing past-role evidence tagged companies with motions they had abandoned.
- Allowing tags without quotes made the column unfalsifiable — nobody could check it, so
  nobody trusted it.
- Allowing a trace tier meant nearly every account carried four or five tags and the field
  stopped separating anything.
- Allowing "unknown" as a value hid the difference between *we looked and found nothing* and
  *we did not look properly*.

The strictness is what makes a filled field worth reading.
